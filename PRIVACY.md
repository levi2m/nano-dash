# Privacy

Nano Dash collects nothing and sends nothing. Neither the phone app nor the watch app declares an internet permission, so they cannot upload anything. CI fails a release if either does.

- **What it records:** your GPS track, phone motion sensors and pressure, and the camera's Bluetooth commands with their times, for the rides you start. A ride's `meta.json` also notes the phone maker and model, the Android and app versions and the exact start and end times; it holds no coordinates. A camera is named in the ride files by the last five characters of its Bluetooth address.
- **Where it stays:** in the app's own folder on your phone (`Android/data/<app>/files/rides`), so you can reach it over USB when troubleshooting. On Android 10 and older, another app that holds storage permission can read, change or add files in that folder; on Android 11 and later other apps cannot. Rides hold precise locations, so treat the files as personal data. Backups and device transfers are switched off for the app.
- **When it leaves:** only when you tap Share on a ride, or tap its route to open it in a maps app, and choose where to send it. The route is handed over as a GPX file; "Check for updates" only opens a web page in your browser. The app itself never uses the network.
- **Deleting:** Delete ride removes the folder and the temporary share copies (the route GPX and the export zip), which are kept in the app's cache until you delete a ride or Android clears the cache. Uninstalling the app removes every ride.
- **Bluetooth:** scanning is used only to find the camera, not your location. The phone's pairing identifier is stored in the app's private preferences with the camera's address.
- **The ride notification:** while a ride runs (or the watch remote is on) the app shows a notification with its state and a Stop or Start button. Android, through the Wear OS companion app on a paired watch, may mirror that notification to the watch. That is the system's feature, not something the app sends; it carries only what the notification shows.
- **The credit link:** the app's footer has a link that opens https://github.com/levi2m in your browser, only when you tap it. The app itself makes no request.
- **The watch:** if you switch on "Quick start from the watch", the phone listens over Bluetooth for the paired watch you chose, only while that switch is on. The link is Bluetooth only, to the watch you chose, with no network involved. It tells the watch whether a ride is running, for how long, and whether GPS and the camera are fine. No locations or files go to the watch. The watch app remembers which paired phone it last connected to, in its own private storage, and the phone app keeps the time the watch last connected (to show "Connected before") in its private preferences. Backups are off for the watch app too. The watch app asks only for Bluetooth connect and vibration.
